OneTap Bharat

Public transport proposal for Mumbai.

India leads the world in digital payments, but a Mumbai commuter still can't tap one card and ride everything. Riding Sydney's Opal network every day, I designed a proposal to give Mumbai one card for every train, Metro and bus, with a weekly limit on what you pay.

See the solution

Estimated cost to build

OneTap Bharat₹165–435 crore
Sydney's Opal network₹8,200 crore

The technology is the smaller cost. The real decision is how much to subsidise the fare discount.

Source: OneTap Bharat proposal. The OneTap bar shows the top of the estimated range.

  • Followed one real commute, Virar to Ghatkopar, across train, Metro and bus: three payment systems, two or three apps, and about ₹452 a week with no cap.
  • Benchmarked Sydney's Opal fare rules and costs, then designed a fare layer for Mumbai: ₹5 off for switching modes, a ₹350 weekly cap, and rail never costing more than the season pass.
  • Estimated the build at ₹165–435 crore against Opal's ₹8,200 crore, showing the real decision is the discount subsidy, not the technology.
  • Planned a four-phase rollout and risk response built around a revenue-sharing agreement with Indian Railways.

    Published research, December 2024

    An anomaly detection system that stops redundant downloads on cloud platforms. It reads download requests, user behaviour and file metadata to tell intentional downloads from accidental repeats, cutting storage costs and wasted bandwidth.

    The paper introduces an intelligent system that detects and prevents unnecessary file downloads. By monitoring download requests, user actions and related metadata, it spots patterns suggesting files are being downloaded more than once by mistake.
